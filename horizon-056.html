Candidate glaciers. A perennial snow bucket uses monthly climate, relief, slope aspect and potential melt. Accumulation needs ≥5 mm water equivalent at each month end, ≥8 mm annual surplus, firn supply and at least two connected 0.25° samples spanning ≥60 km². Thickness tapers to zero at unsupported cardinal edges. The bed graph routes 56% of remaining annual support to the steepest cardinal downhill neighbor. Loss, upstream area, terrain and temperature limit tongues. Thickness uses a 70 kPa driving-stress scale with a 3.5% slope floor and a 500 m cap. Resolution limits small valley glaciers. This diagnostic estimates neither velocity nor time evolution, and stores no water from the ocean budget.

Illustrative volcanic scenario. Mantle forcing helps generate crust enrichment, so the overlap screen provides correlated evidence. The inherited geology has no mapped vents, dated eruptions or exposed volcanic lithologies. The scenario places at most 14 hypothetical sources, ≥360 km apart, where elevated screening overlaps dry land below 68° latitude, at least 280 m above sea datum, and with ≥0.8% local slope. Sites need three contiguous downhill nodes. A synthetic composition index uses 55% crust enrichment and 45% seeded variation, assigning evolved lava above 0.85 and mafic lava elsewhere. Evolved lava starts at a 30 m thickness scale and mafic lava at 18 m. Routes stop at uphill terrain or modeled water, with a 0.65 km step and at most 22 mafic or 12 evolved nodes. Proximal tephra tapers within 2.8 km. Relative age and composition are scenario assignments. Rain and age increase the weathering index. Deposit thickness is a surface field with no bed uplift.

Ecological constraints. Persistent snow and ice cover the modeled soil and reduce plant suitability to zero. Simulated lava and tephra cap effective soil and plant suitability by material and weathering. The original soil and sediment ledger remain available beneath these fields. Seasonal snow is a separate monthly condition.

Ecological composition

Choose an Ecology layer on either map. Planet estimates use the half-degree grid and climate-derived substrate. Regional estimates add resolved soils, sediment, water access and coastal conditions. Tap a sample for fractions and drivers.

Canopy, shrubs, herbs and moss may overlap vertically. The six ground materials partition exposed ground, including underneath vegetation. Stem density counts modeled trees per hectare. Maturity and disturbance pressure are relative indices inferred from rooting, drought, slope, saturation and exposure. Tree groups are fractions of modeled stems. The model uses inferred soil and grain properties, tidal salinity and relative eruption age. Stand age, species identities and sediment grain sizes are unresolved. The volcanic scenario switch also changes ecology.

How to read the refinement

Rivers use continuous centerlines derived from the inherited routed river segments, with discharge-dependent widths, stage and a bounded bank/terrace profile; standing water boundaries otherwise use inherited wetness contours. This is a geometric approximation, not a rerun of lake and river routing. Dry slopes develop small aligned erosion hollows, rock-controlled roughness and gentle sediment mounds. Refinement is pinned to inherited terrain vertices and is deterministic across fine chunks. Features with no inherited water connection are not invented. Routed reach geometry remains coarse (approx. kilometer-scale). River centerline smoothing and local bank cross sections are plausible interpolants, not measured channel migration, sediment flux, or full fluid hydraulics. Unmapped minor waterways retain approximate water contours; lake shorelines and tidal zones are still coarse. No groundwater, glacial erosion, migrating dunes or dynamic geomorphic evolution are added.

Planet climate and biome diagnostics

Area weighting uses spherical 0.5° routing cells. Climate comes from the 1° grid with the inherited elevation correction for temperature and adjusted PET. Precipitation and snowfall retain the coarse-grid climate. Land excludes globally inundated cells. P/PET and snow percentages are averages of cell ratios weighted by area. Comparison groups can differ in latitude, elevation and geography. Local lakes, coasts, soils and habitats are resolved only in generated tiles.

Technical notes and saved terrain

This experiment retains Horizon 030's underlying geological elevation field. Each rock elevation is compared with the original calculated sea level. Above this level, only elevation beyond the mountain threshold is amplified quadratically; below it, only depth beyond the ocean threshold is amplified. Each side has its own curvature strength. The modified surface is used to recalculate sea level from the selected water inventory, then climate and global drainage. This is a deliberately artificial landform exaggeration, not simulated tectonic uplift or trench subduction; it cannot create missing ridges and valleys. Ocean volume is conserved, but coastline and land coverage may change.

The global elevation summary uses the interpolated 0.5° routing grid; it cannot find individual mountain summits or resolve 100 km landforms at high fidelity. The strongest-relief diagnostic samples land elevation at 25 and 50 km geodesic distances in twelve compass directions, avoiding latitude distortion. It is still a coarse-grid estimate, not a measured local mountain range.

The global 0.5° routing graph supplies shared boundary stages and mean flows. Geographic tiles use terrain-weighted routing and discharge-dependent channel cross sections. Small channels become visible in Channel hierarchy. The polar caps use a separate 50 km grid with terrain and environmental fields. Polar channel refinement is pending. Land below sea datum retains the 037 inundation rule. Horizon 040 lake footprints, beds and annual stages carry forward. The annual budget uses land runoff, liquid rainfall on the lake, potential evaporation and 120 mm/year seepage. Monthly net input uses the annual footprint and precedes overflow. It estimates neither changing stage nor seasonal release. Sampled shorelines can leave an annual residual. Spill-capable lakes release water only where a cardinal outlet reaches a lower routed channel or resolved coastal water. Outlet search allows a 0.5 m notch at the shore and at most 5 m of stage incision at a channel join. The solver covers tile-interior basins. Detailed discharge removes intercepted runoff and returns verified overflow once. Shared gates still use coarse-world stages and flows. Lake corrections end at the local tile and do not change neighboring inflows or the planetary graph. The sediment inventory uses the inherited geometric cuts as one-time sources. It traces downhill samples within a tile into identified lake catchments or displayed channels. Lakes trap delivered material up to the modeled bed capacity. Excess stays unplaced in the lake tally. Lake outlets carry water only. Sediment can leave a tile at a routed shared land gate or a verified marine mouth. The next tile receives no sediment imports. Material without a supported route or deposit site stays in the unplaced tally. Deposit thickness uses 35% pore space and fits within the inherited terrain envelope. Soil is part of the weathered mantle. The rock enrichment and sand/gravel versus silt/clay fractions are broad composition proxies. The ledger covers a single generation pass with no time interval. Shallow beds and low banks use fixed settling fractions and thickness caps. Grain fractions track the source mixture. Inherited bank and coastal grading is reported separately from routed sediment. The probe shows the local weathered mantle.

Coastal terrain uses nearby slopes, a composition-based rock-strength proxy, eight sampled wave-fetch directions out to 96 km, and rainfall and relief as an erosion proxy. Sheltered shores use a synthetic tidal half-range of 0.8–2.6 m. The tide proxy limits where intertidal terrain is indicated. Land and seabed are regraded near sea level while preserving wet and dry footprints. The inherited river-mouth landform proxy follows the channel direction and tapers within 4.5 km. Routed deposits in this version are limited to the verified mouth sample. The coastal pass keeps the global sea datum and annual lake and river budgets. Fine shoreline smoothing uses a local sample-weighted contour and changes only the display-level geometry; it does not update sea-volume or sediment budgets. Its local seabed adjustments are outside the global water inventory calculation. The grid resolves broad beach and dune terrain and potential marsh substrate. Individual dunes, marsh vegetation, surge, alongshore transport, tidal circulation and estuary mixing are unresolved.

Horizon 046 uses the sea-level temperature gradient and seasonal cycle introduced in 045 for an Earth-like climate. Ocean influence weakens with distance inland on the 1° climate grid. Elevation cools land by 6.2 °C/km. Monthly temperatures feed evaporation demand, atmospheric moisture capacity, rain and snow partitioning, runoff and the biome estimates. This model approximates maritime moderation and has no ocean-current heat transport.

Climate biomes estimate potential vegetation from monthly temperature, precipitation, snow and evaporation demand. The planet overview uses coarse climate and elevation. Detail adjusts temperature for local altitude and maps habitat overrides from the final land surface, coast, substrate and inland water. Riparian wetness and low height above nearby water are proxies for a shallow water table. The classification has no groundwater, glacier, plant population or succession simulation. Local habitat detail is limited by grid spacing and the extent of the terrain and hydrology models.
